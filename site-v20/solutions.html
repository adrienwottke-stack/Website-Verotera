GUIDED TOUR · STAGE 1 OF 5The path starts with your requirement. Our example is a traction inverter on an 800-volt vehicle platform. Before any hardware is designed, the Engineering Core checks the requirement for feasibility, conflicts and missing information. It applies fixed rules: an 800-volt bus calls for 1200-volt devices in a two-level design. Where information is missing, such as the mission profile for thermal cycling, it asks instead of guessing. The result is a design basis that every later decision can refer to.

What the Engineering Core returns

Hard constraint

An 800 V bus with transients requires 1200 V class devices in a two-level topology. 650 V classes only in multilevel arrangements.

Trade-off

Switching frequency against efficiency, EMC effort and magnetics, documented as an open decision.

Escalation

Mission profile missing. The thermal-cycling assessment is requested from you, not estimated.

Result: a design basis with rule, evidence level and source for every statement.

How the verdict is formed is shown on the Engineering Core page.

Gate drive

Optical Gate Driving

Light-activated gate control removes the gate bond wires: no bond-wire inductance in the gate path and inherent galvanic isolation. The aim: cleaner switching, balanced parallel chips and lower EMI.
